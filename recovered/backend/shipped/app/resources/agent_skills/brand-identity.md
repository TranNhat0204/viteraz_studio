# Brand identity layer

Every master prompt must carry this. Without it a script produces a channel nobody can name after four videos — the viewer remembers the case and forgets the show.

The failure in the other direction is worse: a fixed tagline recited every episode reads as a template, and templated repetition across a catalog is exactly the signal platform enforcement uses to identify mass-produced content.

---

## The three tiers of recognition

Brand recognition in a narration channel is built in three layers, in descending order of power. Most channels invert this and repeat the name eight times while having no voice at all.

**TIER 1 — Recognition through content structure.**
The named recurring segment only this channel does: the document beat, the element-by-element, the three levers, the mechanism block, the line-by-line. A regular viewer starts waiting for it. This is simultaneously a retention device and the strongest brand asset available.

**TIER 2 — Recognition through narrator behavior.**
The register and its rules: the plainer sentence at the more disturbing fact, the tense discipline, the refusal to speculate, the acknowledged gap. A returning viewer should identify the channel from two minutes of narration with the name removed. If they cannot, no amount of naming will fix it.

**TIER 3 — Direct naming.**
The channel name, spoken twice. This is the smallest tier by design, and the one most master prompts omit entirely. Tiers 1 and 2 without Tier 3 produce a channel with a strong voice and weak recall.

A master prompt strong on Tiers 1 and 2 with no Tier 3 module scores roughly eight out of ten on brand. Adding the module below takes it to nine and a half without making the video feel like an advertisement.

## The brand compass

Each channel gets one internal philosophy line — the sentence that settles editorial arguments.

- 33 Cold Case: *The case ends where the record ends.*
- 34 The Verdict: *The jury only heard part of it. Show which part.*
- 35 Fraud Files: *The target was competent. The con was industrial.*
- 36 Memory Lane: *Warmth is the tone. Documentation is the product.*
- 37 What Happened To: *Careers end for structural reasons. Find the structure.*
- 38 Money After 50: *Work the number, both ways, then get out of the way.*
- 39 Family Secrets: *The object carries the feeling. Say less.*
- 40 Declassified: *Only what the file says. Name the gap.*
- 41 The Diagnosis: *Explain the body, not the miracle.*
- 42 Second Chances: *Hope is a detail, not a claim.*
- 43 Final Report: *Explain the system before it fails.*
- 44 Rise and Fall: *One decision. Follow it to the number.*
- 45 AI and Your Money: *Deflate the claim. Hand them something to do.*
- 46 Relocation: *Count everything, including the line nobody prints.*
- 47 Lights Went Out: *Competence, not fear. Free lessons first.*

Rule: the compass is internal. It shapes every episode and is never recited verbatim on air. If it surfaces in narration at all, it surfaces rephrased, once, and never twice the same way.

## Placement rules for Tier 3

**Never in the first 40 seconds.** The hook window is the steepest drop on the retention curve. A channel name there is a measurable leak. The name is earned after the viewer has decided to stay.

**THE BRAND BRIDGE — once, between 0:40 and 1:30, 8 to 12 seconds spoken.**

Three jobs, two or three sentences, then out:
1. name the channel,
2. state the editorial promise, phrased fresh for this episode's subject,
3. return immediately to the story.

Not a greeting. Not "welcome back". Not a subscribe pitch. Written well it reads as a documentary stating its own terms.

Shapes, not scripts — generate new wording every episode:
- "This is [CHANNEL]. Here we stay with what the record can prove, and with what it still cannot explain."
- "You are watching [CHANNEL], where the case file matters more than the theory."
- "[CHANNEL] takes trials apart the way a jury has to. This one turned on a single ruling."

**MID-EPISODE — optional, at most one, only at a section seam.**
Forbidden inside the emotional peak, inside the signature beat, inside evidence or testimony, and inside the final two minutes before the close. If there is no natural seam, skip it. A missing mid-mention costs nothing; an intrusive one costs the moment.

**THE SIGNATURE — once, in the final 30 seconds.**
Function: hand the episode back to the audience and stamp it. Wording varies every episode; function never does.

- "You have been watching [CHANNEL]."
- "That is where the record stands. This is [CHANNEL]."
- "This is [CHANNEL]. Thank you for staying with it."

**Budget: two mentions per episode. Three is the absolute ceiling.** More is self-promotion and this audience feels it immediately.

## Signature closing logic

The last ninety seconds follow a fixed sequence with variable wording:

1. CURRENT STATUS — where the documented story actually stands right now.
2. WHAT REMAINS UNANSWERED — the honest gap, stated plainly.
3. ONE CASE-SPECIFIC QUESTION — arguable, answerable either way, tied to a detail from this episode.
4. CHANNEL SIGNATURE — the name, once, naturally.
5. OPTIONAL FINAL LINE — one original sentence under fifteen words that leaves residue. Skip it if the question is already the strongest ending.

Hard rule: never the same sign-off twice. The swap test applies to the whole closing block — if it could end ten unrelated episodes, it is rewritten.

---

## Calls to action

- One per episode, maximum.
- Placed at a value moment: after the payload beat, or attached to the closing question. Never in the first two minutes.
- Form: an invitation to answer the specific question. Never "smash that subscribe button".
- On the service channels (fraud, money, health, housing, survival), the strongest CTA is a share instruction with a reason: "if someone in your family is in this bracket, send them this one."
- Never stack like, subscribe, bell, and membership. That stack is the visual signature of the mill tier.

## The module every master prompt must contain

Reproduce this shape, filled with the channel's own material, placed immediately after the CHANNEL DNA section:

```
==================================================
4B. BRAND IDENTITY AND PLACEMENT
==================================================

CHANNEL NAME: {{CHANNEL_NAME}}

BRAND COMPASS (internal, never recited verbatim):
[the channel's compass line]

The audience should recognize {{CHANNEL_NAME}} first through editorial
behavior, and only then through the name.

TIER 1 - the named recurring segment: [name it]
TIER 2 - the narrator rules: [two or three lines]
TIER 3 - direct naming, restrained, as follows.

1. Do NOT mention the channel in the first 40 seconds.
2. One BRAND BRIDGE between 0:40 and 1:30, 8 to 12 seconds:
   channel name + editorial promise phrased for THIS episode + return to the story.
   Generate fresh wording every episode. Never reuse a sentence.
   [two example shapes, marked as shapes]
3. Optional single mid-episode mention, at a section seam only.
   Forbidden during evidence, testimony, the signature beat, or the emotional peak.
4. One signature mention in the final 30 seconds. Vary the wording.
5. Maximum TWO mentions per episode, THREE absolute ceiling.
6. One CTA maximum, attached to the closing question.

The channel name should read as the signature on the work,
not as an advertisement.
```

And an audit line in the final-audit section:

> Could a returning viewer identify this channel from two minutes of narration with the name removed? If no, the identity is decorative — fix the register, not the mention count.
